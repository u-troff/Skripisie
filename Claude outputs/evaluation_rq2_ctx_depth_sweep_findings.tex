\paragraph{Context window and the plan ceiling.} The context sweep was re-run and
extended from depth 40 out to depth 120 (three runs per depth, every four depths
from 4 to 120, 90 runs per context setting, 270 runs in total). Figure~\ref{fig:ctx}
shows the extended sweep. No hallucination was recorded in any of the 270 runs, at
any depth or context setting: the failure mode throughout is a planner that stops
generating further steps partway through an otherwise-correct, in-order plan
(\texttt{first\_divergence} always lands exactly on the step at which the plan
stops), not one that substitutes a wrong object, location or action. Every context
setting produced a correct plan in all three runs from depth 4 to 28. The first
slip appears at depth 32 for \texttt{num\_ctx}=1024 (2 of 3 correct) and at depth
36 for \texttt{num\_ctx}=8192 (2 of 3 correct); \texttt{num\_ctx}=2048 has no slip
before depth 40. By depth 40, every run at every context setting fails, and none
recovers at any depth tested up to 120. The ceiling is therefore a plan-length
limit of the model's own generation, and not simply a context-window limit: going
from 1024 to 8192 tokens, an eightfold increase, does not move the depth at which
the plan starts to fail. What the larger context window does change is how badly
each run fails once it starts: at depths 40 and above, \texttt{num\_ctx}=1024 and
2048 both produce a completely empty plan in roughly 40\% of runs (26 of 63 and 27
of 63), while \texttt{num\_ctx}=8192 never produces an empty plan and sustains
close to double the mean plan length of the other two settings (46.7 steps against
23.7--24.6). A larger context window does not delay when the plan starts to
degrade here, but it does raise the ceiling on how much of a degrading plan
survives.

\paragraph{RQ2.} In the virtual tests no hallucinated step appeared at any depth
tested, including the extended context sweep run out to 120 chained instructions:
across all 270 of those runs, the planner never substitutes a wrong object,
location or action. Compositional depth does have a hard limit, but the failure
mode is truncation, not hallucination: from depth 40 onward, every run at every
context setting tested (1024, 2048 and 8192 tokens) stops generating steps
partway through an otherwise-correct plan, and none recovers at any depth up to
120. Context window size does not move this onset, but it does change its
severity: the smaller context settings produce a completely empty plan in
roughly 40\% of deep runs, while the largest setting never does and sustains
close to double the plan length on average, which points to a generation-length
ceiling in the planner's own decoding behaviour rather than the model running
out of context-window capacity to track the room and the command. Neither this
truncation nor the silent dropping of unresolvable steps described above is
caught by the pipeline at plan time, which argues for a capability and
coverage check on the plan before departure as the most useful next improvement.
